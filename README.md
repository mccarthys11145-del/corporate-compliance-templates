# Corporate compliance templates

Editable working documents for defining responsibility and maintaining the evidence behind a compliance program. The examples are illustrative. They contain no client records, adopted company policy, actual approvals, or invented compliance deadlines.

| File | Use |
| --- | --- |
| [Compliance policy](compliance-policy-template.md) | Set scope, authority mapping, ownership, controls, escalation, exceptions, and review |
| [Board responsibility matrix](board-responsibility-matrix.csv) | Assign one accountable role and the responsible operators for each activity |
| [Compliance risk register](compliance-risk-register.csv) | Capture a risk, governing obligation, control, evidence, residual assessment, and action |
| [Evidence checklist](evidence-checklist.md) | Define what must support an assessment and a closure decision |
| [Review log](review-log.csv) | Preserve dated findings, decisions, and action ownership |

## Adopt the templates

1. Copy the files into the organization's controlled document location. Replace bracketed policy fields with actual scope, owners, approval authority, and review arrangements.
2. Map requirements to primary authority or an adopted internal policy. State which business process and jurisdiction each requirement covers.
3. Replace the illustrative responsibility allocations with the organization's approved delegation. Record accountable (`A`), responsible (`R`), consulted (`C`), and informed (`I`) roles. Each activity has one `A`; more than one `R` is possible.
4. Replace `EXAMPLE-*` rows with actual register records. Assign ownership and rating definitions before scoring. Blank ratings and dates mean unassessed or unassigned, not zero risk.
5. Use the evidence checklist for assessment and closure. Record each substantive review as a new log row and retain prior decisions.

## Risk assessment method

The register deliberately leaves likelihood and impact blank. Adopt a scale suited to the organization and document its definitions in the policy before using it. Rate inherent risk before considering the listed controls and residual risk after evaluating their design and operation. Avoid treating an undocumented control as effective.

No numerical risk score or color threshold is preselected. Record the rationale and evidence location so another reviewer can reconstruct the assessment. `target_date` is an internal action date chosen by the organization; if an actual legal deadline governs, cite that authority and distinguish the legal date from the internal target.

## Editing CSVs

Open or import the CSVs in a spreadsheet editor, or edit them as UTF-8 text. Preserve header names and standard CSV quoting. Use `YYYY-MM-DD` dates when completed; import those columns as text to preserve that literal format. The plain CSV format stores values without formulas, formatting, access controls, or validation rules.

Keep evidence files in the organization's appropriate repository and record references in the register. Public repositories should contain blank or illustrative templates; operational registers and sensitive evidence belong in the organization's controlled environment.

## Completion criteria

An adopted policy has a defined scope and approval record. Each active risk has an owner, an identified requirement, an assessment rationale, and a review status. An implemented action has evidence and a recorded verification result. A closed issue has an authorized decision and any remaining follow-up clearly assigned.
